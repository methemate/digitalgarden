### How Many Clients Can Financial Advisors Really Serve?
- **Published:** November 23, 2026
- **Category:** Practice Management

Drawing on Kitces Research on Advisor Wellbeing, this post argues that the right client count depends on whether an advisor is a "minder" (about 71% of advisors, who prefer deepening existing relationships) or a "finder" (who enjoys business development). Minder wellbeing tends to peak around 50-100 clients and then falls, while finder wellbeing keeps rising past 200 clients. Client complexity matters too, since annual service hours climb sharply for higher-revenue clients. Minders may do best by capping growth or "averaging up" to fewer, more affluent clients, while finders may prefer "sizing up" with more staff and capacity.

### Practical Conversations To Help Clients Plan Through AI Uncertainty, From Portfolios To Career Risk
- **Published:** November 18, 2026
- **Category:** Client Trust & Communication

Guest author Zach Bennedsen looks at how advisors can talk with clients about the very wide range of possible AI outcomes. Diversified portfolios already carry a lot of AI exposure through the largest index stocks, which helps enthusiasts but makes AI hard for skeptics to avoid (direct indexing or purpose-built ETFs are options). For working clients the bigger risk may be to their careers and future earnings, which can justify larger emergency reserves. For clients stuck on the uncertainty, the post suggests choosing actions that limit the worst regret instead of trying to predict how AI plays out.
