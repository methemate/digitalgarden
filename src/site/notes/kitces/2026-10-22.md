### Beyond Google Reviews: A Two-Pronged Online Review Approach Balancing Marketing Impact With Compliance
- **Published:** October 21, 2026
- **Category:** Marketing, Regulation & Compliance
- **Status:** Earlier

Whit Lanier (Amplify Reviews) and Kent Keister (Reliant Compliance) explain how advisors can use client reviews without creating a compliance headache. They cite a 2026 report finding that 79% of top RIAs still don't use testimonials or online reviews, often out of compliance concerns, even though prospects rely on reviews and Google boosts highly rated businesses. Receiving reviews is rarely the problem. Soliciting them is: firms must ask all clients (not just happy ones), document the process, and use rating systems that allow favorable and unfavorable feedback equally. Because Google reviews can't be anonymous, many clients won't post them, so the authors suggest pairing Google with third-party anonymous testimonials. Relying on only one channel means giving up either reach or client participation.

### Winning Clients And Moving 'Upmarket' By Being Confident In Telling Your Value Story: #FASuccess Ep 512 With Chris Smith
- **Published:** October 20, 2026
- **Category:** Financial Advisor Success Podcast
- **Status:** Earlier

Chris Smith, founder of brand strategy firm PSBLTY, describes a discovery process built on leadership. In the first meeting the advisor mostly listens and reflects back what they hear, and closes with an "assertive assumption" instead of asking whether there are any questions. The second meeting is where the advisor shares planning insights and a strategy, without going so deep that the prospect gets confused, and then uses scripts to invite them to become a client. He also discusses growing versus scaling a business and tracking key metrics.

### Why Profitability Matters For Client Service: Using Per-Client Revenue To Identify When To 'Graduate' Clients
- **Published:** October 19, 2026
- **Category:** Practice Management
- **Status:** Earlier

Guest authors Matthew Jarvis and Micah Shilanski show how to measure a firm's true profitability: divide "True Net" (money reaching the owner's tax return) by "True Gross" (everything clients pay), aiming for at least 30% and ideally 50%. They then sort clients with a Red/Yellow/Green revenue report. Green clients stay, yellow clients get an "upgrade or graduate" conversation, and red clients (with a few exceptions) are graduated to a better-fit advisor with an authentic explanation.
