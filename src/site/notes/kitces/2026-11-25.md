### Adding Tax Preparation In-House Or With External Partners: Strategy, Operations, and Legal/Compliance Issues To Consider
- **Published:** November 25, 2026
- **Category:** Regulation & Compliance

Guest authors Russell Kroeger and Chris Stanley lay out three ways advisory firms can bring tax return preparation closer to their planning work: partnering with an outside tax professional, preparing returns in-house, or acquiring a tax firm. Partnering is the lightest lift but gives less control, while in-house preparation needs credentialed staff, tracking systems, and a choice between running it through the RIA or a separate affiliate. Acquisitions bring talent and clients quickly but carry cultural friction and possible change-of-control issues. The point is that these options sit on one spectrum of cost, control, and complexity, so firms can start small and move along it over time.

### How Many Clients Can Financial Advisors Really Serve?
- **Published:** November 23, 2026
- **Category:** Practice Management

Drawing on Kitces Research on Advisor Wellbeing, this piece argues the right client count depends on whether an advisor is a "minder" (about 71%, who prefer deepening existing relationships) or a "finder" (who enjoy business development). Minder wellbeing peaks around 50-100 clients and then declines, while finder wellbeing keeps rising past 200. Client complexity matters too, since higher-revenue clients need far more service hours. Minders may do best by capping growth or "averaging up" to fewer, higher-revenue clients, while finders can keep "sizing up" with more staff and efficiency.
